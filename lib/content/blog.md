# **How Many Cold Emails Should You Send Per Day Before You Get Flagged as Spam?**

Most cold email domains should start at 15 to 20 emails per day on a brand-new domain and scale up gradually, while a well-established domain with a clean sending history can typically handle 50 to 150 emails per day per inbox before risking spam flags. The exact ceiling isn't a fixed number so much as a function of your bounce rate and spam complaint rate, which is what inbox providers actually watch.

## **Why Sending Limits Exist in the First Place**

Gmail, Yahoo, and Microsoft don't cap your sending based on an arbitrary daily number. They cap it based on engagement signals: how many of your emails bounce, how many recipients mark you as spam, and how quickly you ramped up volume on a new domain. Gmail and Yahoo's bulk sender requirements, in effect since February 2024, require proper SPF, DKIM, and DMARC authentication, a one-click unsubscribe option, and a spam complaint rate that stays under roughly 0.3 percent. Microsoft rolled out similar authentication and volume requirements for Outlook, Hotmail, and Live accounts starting May 2025\. Cross either threshold and your emails start landing in spam regardless of how good your copy is.

## **Recommended Sending Limits by Domain Age**

| Domain Age | Recommended Daily Volume | Notes |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| New domain (0–2 weeks) | 5–15 per day | Warm-up only; prioritize replies over volume |
| Warming (2–6 weeks) | 15–40 per day, increasing \~5–10/week | Check bounce rate weekly before scaling further |
| Established (6+ weeks, clean history) | 50–150 per day, per inbox | Scale total volume by adding inboxes, not by pushing one inbox harder |
| Any domain, bounce rate above 3–5% | Pause sending | Fix the list before you fix the schedule |

## **The Real Bottleneck Usually Isn't Volume**

The single biggest cause of a high bounce rate isn't sending too fast. It's sending to a bad list. A tightly targeted, verified list of 300 contacts will protect your domain reputation better than an unverified list of 3,000, because bounce rate and spam complaint rate are percentages, not raw counts. A bad list makes every other part of a sending strategy harder to execute, no matter how carefully you ramp volume.

## **A Simple Weekly Ramp You Can Follow**

**Week 1:** 10–15 emails/day, focus entirely on deliverability, not replies. **Week 2:** 20–25 emails/day if bounce rate stays under 3%. **Week 3:** 30–40 emails/day, monitor spam complaint rate closely. **Week 4 onward:** 50+ emails/day per inbox if metrics stay clean; add a second inbox to scale further rather than pushing one inbox past 150/day.
